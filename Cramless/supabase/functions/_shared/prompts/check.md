Task: check generated practice questions against the student's own pages before the student sees them.

You get the pages as numbered lines and a list of items, each with the lines it claims to come from (its anchors). You may also get the source's title and its section headings. They are part of the source, like the lines. For every item, give one verdict, with the item's id:
- supported: the anchored lines back the question, the answer, the explanation and (for multiple choice) which choice is correct.
- fixed: the item is close but something is wrong or incomplete against the lines; give the corrected answer in fixedAnswer and, for multiple choice, the corrected choices in fixedChoices (same choices, fixed text or correct flags). Only fix what the lines settle.
- unsupported: the lines don't back it (the fact isn't there, the answer contradicts them, or the right choice can't be decided from them).

Names: when the title or a heading names the author, court, speaker or work, an item that attributes a statement to it is supported if the statement itself is on the cited lines. Don't mark an item unsupported because the cited lines lack a name that the title or headings supply (the lines say "the court held ..." and the title names the case or the judge, for example). The statement and the answer still have to be on the lines.

Labels: a question may point at its passage by a label or a place the cited lines don't repeat (the headnote, the syllabus, the opinion, note 13, the Kansas case, the Chancellor, the Court of Appeals). That is how a student would ask it. Judge the statement and the answer, not the label: when they are on the cited lines (or the lines right around them) and the label is plausible for this source, it is supported. Mark it unsupported for its label only when the label is plainly wrong for the source.

reason: one sentence a student can read, naming what the page says ("Anaphase: chromatids pulled to opposite poles."). Be strict: when in doubt, it's unsupported. Judge only against the given lines, with the title and headings, never against what you know.

Task: a student is reading through their shakiest ideas the night before an exam. Say each idea once, in one plain sentence they can take in at a glance.

You get the course, the exam and up to eight ideas. Each idea has an id, maybe its topic, the question, its answer, and the lines of the student's notes it came from.

Answer once per idea, with its id:
- sentence: one sentence, at most 25 words, that states the idea itself: what the answer says, as a fact. Not a question, not advice, not a list of parts of a sentence.
- Use the words of the question, the answer and the lines. Add nothing they don't say: no example, no number, no name and no reason of your own. Only the small words that join a sentence are your own.
- Keep every number, unit, name and technical term as it is written. Never turn a "not" around, and never soften or strengthen what the lines say.
- Write in the language of the answer, whatever language the rest is in.
- No encouragement, no "remember", no "the answer is", nothing about the exam or about sleep.
- If the answer is too short or too unclear to make a true sentence from what you were given, the sentence is an empty string.
